Who we are

Every child deserves to learn in a way that fits how their mind actually works.

Pholoso Afrika Foundation is a South African registered non-profit company. We advance inclusive, adaptive, learner-centred support for neurodivergent children and learners, across schools, communities and families.

Our conviction

Difference is not deficit.

Neurodivergent learners are not defined by limitation. They are defined by different ways of thinking, communicating, processing information and engaging with the world.

Education systems should respond to that diversity. Learners should not have to bend themselves to fit rigid, standardised structures. The structures should learn to fit the learner.

Our mission

What we are here to do.

To advance inclusive, adaptive, learner-centred educational support systems for neurodivergent children. We pursue this through advocacy, innovation, collaboration and community-driven engagement.

Our vision

The future we are building.

A future in which neurodivergent children across South Africa and Africa are genuinely understood, supported and empowered. Built on education designed to recognise cognitive diversity rather than suppress it.

The Hlompho principle
A child's passion is not a side interest. It is something to be honoured, because it may be the clearest doorway into how they learn.

Hlompho means respect, honour, and reverence. For us, it begins with the belief that every child carries something worthy of being seen before they are assessed, corrected, or asked to adapt. We begin with what the child loves, because interests often reveal how they think, communicate, engage and make sense of the world. Learning becomes more personal, more humane, and more capable of unlocking meaningful participation.

Learner-centred, in action

Start with how a child learns, and the curriculum opens up.

A child's interest is not a distraction from learning. Used well, it is a doorway to all of it. Choose one, and see.

Trains as the curriculum

Numeracy

Timetables, platform numbers and journey times become counting, telling time and sequencing.

Language & literacy

Station names, route maps and train stories build vocabulary, reading and narrative.

Science & the world

How engines move, electricity and momentum open physics, engineering and geography.

Social & emotional

Sharing the interest with a friend or teacher builds turn-taking, joint attention and trust.

Dinosaurs as the curriculum

Numeracy

Sorting by size, counting bones and millions of years build number, scale and estimation.

Language & literacy

Long Latin names and fact books grow reading confidence and specialist vocabulary.

Science & the world

Extinction, fossils and habitats open biology, geology and deep time.

Social & emotional

Teaching others dinosaur facts builds communication, leadership and pride.

Music as the curriculum

Numeracy

Beats, bars and rhythm are fractions, patterns and counting you can feel.

Language & literacy

Lyrics and song stories build phonics, memory and expression.

Science & the world

How sound and vibration travel opens physics and the body.

Social & emotional

Playing or singing together builds timing, listening and belonging.

Numbers as the curriculum

Numeracy

A love of numbers is mathematics already. Extend it into algebra, data and logic.

Language & literacy

Explaining how a sum works builds reasoning and clear, precise language.

Science & the world

Measurement, coding and patterns connect numbers to the real world.

Social & emotional

Number games with others build patience, turn-taking and shared joy.

Art as the curriculum

Numeracy

Shape, symmetry, proportion and perspective are geometry in colour.

Language & literacy

Telling the story behind a drawing builds narrative and vocabulary.

Science & the world

Mixing colour, light and materials opens chemistry and physics.

Social & emotional

Sharing and displaying art builds confidence and connection.

Football as the curriculum

Numeracy

Scores, league tables and angles become arithmetic, data and geometry.

Language & literacy

Match reports and player stories build reading and writing.

Science & the world

How the body moves and the ball flies opens biology and physics.

Social & emotional

Team play is turn-taking, rules and friendship in motion.

Professional voice

“I find the bug nobody else can see. Then I am asked why I did not smile in the meeting.”

Naledi, 29, software tester

What the world often misunderstands
That being quiet, literal or unsmiling means I am not capable or not a team player.
Where the system creates friction
Open-plan noise drains me by 10am. Interviews score small talk, not the skill I was hired to use.
What helps me take part
Written instructions, noise-cancelling headphones, a quiet corner, and being judged on my work.
The shift this asks for
Hire for the skill. Redesign interviews and offices so focus is possible.
Parent voice

“We waited three years for a school place. Sipho waited at home, with all his curiosity and nowhere to put it.”

Thandi, mother of Sipho, 7

What the world often misunderstands
That a child who does not speak much is not learning. He learns constantly. Trains, numbers, patterns.
Where the system creates friction
Long waiting lists, no support while we wait, and a system that sees a label before it sees my son.
What helps me take part
Early, learner-centred teaching that starts from what he loves. And someone walking beside our family.
The shift this asks for
Build more places, and help families learn at home while they wait.
Young voice

“I know the answer. I just cannot show it the way the exam hall wants me to.”

Lwazi, 16, learner

What the world often misunderstands
That needing things to be predictable means I am difficult. It means I do my best work when I am calm.
Where the system creates friction
Noise in the exam hall, timetable changes with no warning, and being told to make eye contact.
What helps me take part
A quiet space, routine I can trust, my headphones, and a chance to show what I know through my art.
The shift this asks for
Let learners show knowledge in ways that fit how they think.
Educator voice

“I have forty children and no training in neurodiversity. I am improvising, and I am scared of failing them.”

Mr Dube, foundation-phase teacher

What the world often misunderstands
That a struggling class means a lazy teacher. We are under-resourced, not uncaring.
Where the system creates friction
No affirming training, huge classes, and pressure to correct behaviour instead of understanding it.
What helps me take part
Mentoring, affirming teaching support, and tools that work with the child, not against them.
The shift this asks for
Train and support teachers as the front line of inclusion.

    Privacy notice

    How we handle your information

    We collect as little as we can, use it only to help you, and never sell it.

    Draft: this notice is being reviewed and may change. Questions? Email know@pholoso.africa.

    Who we are

    Pholoso Afrika Foundation NPC (NPO 333-132-NPO, CIPC 2025/951168/08, PBO 930090077) is responsible for the personal information collected through this website. You can reach us at know@pholoso.africa.

    What we collect, and why

    • Contact form: your name and email address (both optional), the option you choose under "I am a…", your message, your privacy confirmation, whether the enquiry concerns a child and, if so, your guardian confirmation. We use them only to read and reply to your message.
    • Email updates: your email address and your consent, with the date. We use them only to send the updates you asked for.
    • Spam protection: when you send a form, your internet (IP) address is turned into a scrambled one-way code to limit how many messages one visitor can send in an hour. The address itself is not stored, and timestamps stop counting after an hour. Files are removed during occasional cleanup on later submissions, so they may remain longer when the site is quiet. The code is not an anonymous identifier.
    • Comfort & access settings: saved only in your own browser, on your device. We never receive them.

    Please don't include your child's full name or medical details in a message. We don't need them to help you.

    Cookies and tracking

    The website code does not add cookies, advertising trackers or analytics. The current hosting service adds a third-party monitoring script. We are asking the host to disable it and confirm what it collects. Until that is confirmed, we cannot promise that the live website is free of tracking.

    Who handles your information for us

    • Microsoft 365 delivers and stores the email your message becomes.
    • Our web host runs this website on servers outside South Africa and may keep standard server logs, such as IP address, date and page requested, for security.
    • Read aloud uses the voice built into your own browser or device. Some browsers send the text being read to their own online voice service. That text is our page content, not information about you.

    This means information you send through the website may be processed outside South Africa.

    How long we keep it

    We are confirming our retention periods as part of the review of this notice. Until then, you can ask us at any time to delete your message or to remove you from updates.

    Your rights

    Under the Protection of Personal Information Act (POPIA) you can ask to see the information we hold about you, ask us to correct or delete it, and object to how we use it. You can withdraw your consent to updates at any time. Email know@pholoso.africa.

    If you are not happy with how we handle your information, you can complain to the Information Regulator (South Africa) at inforegulator.org.za (opens external site in a new tab).

    Children

    You don't need to give us any information about a child to use this website. When an enquiry concerns a child, we ask the sender to confirm they are the parent or legal guardian. For a general professional or partnership enquiry, choose "No" and leave out information about individual children.
